The Mathematical Proof

In standard transformer runtimes (HF, vLLM), cache buffers are allocated uniformly for all N_total = 35 layers. Gemma 4 reuses the KV states from Layer 13 & 14 for the remaining 20 layers:

Total Layers: 35 layers
Active KV Layers: 15 layers (L0–L14)
Frozen Shared Layers: 20 layers (L15–L34)
Memory Elimination: 20 / 35 = 57.14%

Why doesn't L15-L34 have W_k and W_v?

Gemma 4 was intentionally trained with Cross-Layer Attention (CLA). Freezing the KV representations at L14 allows deep transformer layers to refine query representations without polluting or bloating the key/value vector memory.

Why does `index_copy_` with a device tensor succeed where Python slicing fails?

When you write buf[:, :, i:i+1] = k, the Python interpreter lowers the integer i as a compile-time constant literal into the StableHLO dynamic-update-slice operator. Because the literal is embedded into the AST graph signature, XLA views step 0 and step 1 as entirely different programs!

By passing pos_tensor = torch.tensor([i], device=device) into buf.index_copy_(2, pos_tensor, k), the slice index becomes a runtime device operand. The tensor shapes, operator topology, and graph metadata remain 100% invariant, locking in 0 recompilations and full hardware throughput.
