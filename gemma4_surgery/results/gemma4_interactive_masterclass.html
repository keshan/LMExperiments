Chapter 1: Dissecting Gemma 4 E2B-it — Interactive 35-Layer MRI

Why we started by reading the exact runtime source code (`modeling_gemma4_exact.py`) and profiling all 35 layers on Colab TPU v5e-1

Exp 0 & Exp 1 • Phase 1 Anatomy

Beginner Intuition: The 35-Floor Building

Standard Transformers are uniform: every layer builds its own memory (Key-Value cache) and runs the same size neural network. Gemma 4 E2B-it is radically asymmetric:

• Floors 0–14 (Active Note-Takers): Compute fresh Keys and Values, with small 4,096-wide MLPs.
• Floors 15–34 (Frozen Memory + Giant Brains): Stop computing Keys and Values! All 20 top floors reuse the frozen notes from Floor 13 (local sliding window) and Floor 14 (global full context), while tripling their MLP width to 12,288.
• The 2.35B Per-Layer Cheat Sheet (PLE): Over half (`50.7%`) of the text model's parameters sit in a giant lookup table that hands every single floor a fresh vector of the current surface token.

Intermediate Systems: Multimodal Wrapper Trap

In Exp 0b, we discovered a critical trap: `unsloth/gemma-4-E2B-it` (`5.03B` total params) is saved under `Gemma4ForConditionalGeneration` (`model.language_model.*`). Loading it with `Gemma4ForCausalLM` silently leaves the text backbone randomly initialized (`layer_scalar = 1.0`)!

By loading `Gemma4ForConditionalGeneration` and setting `vision_tower`, `audio_tower`, `embed_vision`, and `embed_audio` to `None` before moving to TPU, we loaded all 1,951 weights cleanly while freeing 0.80 GB of TPU HBM.

Parameter breakdown of the `4.63B` text model:
• Main token embedding: `402.7M` (`8.7%`)
• PLE lookup table: `2,348.8M` (`50.7%`)
• Active compute layers (`L0–34`): `1,877.1M` (`40.6%`)

Advanced Geometry: L14 Reset & L30–31 Drift

Each decoder layer $\ell \in \{0,\dots,34\}$ updates the residual stream $h_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$ via:
h_mid = h_in + PostAttnNorm(Attn(PreAttnNorm(h_in)))
h_mlp = h_mid + PostMLPNorm(MLP(PreMLPNorm(h_mid)))
u_l = PostPLENorm(W_proj(GELU(W_gate h_mlp) ⊙ p_l(x_t)))
h_out = layer_scalar_l * (h_mlp + u_l)

At L13–L14 (the KV-sharing boundary), $\cos(h_{\text{in}}, h_{\text{out}})$ plunges to `0.034` and `0.024` (`mlp_u = 1941.1`, `ple_post = 491.4`), performing an almost $90^\circ$ basis rotation before freezing the KV cache! Conversely, across L25–L32, $\cos(h_{\text{in}}, h_{\text{out}})$ climbs to `0.982` (`L30`) and `0.981` (`L31`).

Chapter 2: The PLE Norm-Cancellation Theorem & Subspace Projection Simulator

Why naive weight scaling has 0.00% effect, why `post_per_layer_input_norm` clamps surface noise, and how `S1d` fixes it

Discovery 1 • S1a / S1c / S1d

Beginner Intuition: The Automatic Volume Leveler

Suppose a microphone has an Automatic Gain Control (RMSNorm) placed after the volume knob. Even if the model tries to turn the Per-Layer Embedding (PLE) gate down to a whisper (`0.01x`) because the current word is just `"the"` or `"Here"`, the RMSNorm immediately amplifies that whisper back up to full volume!

That is why in Layer 33, the static surface-token injection (`22.88`) is more than twice as loud as contextual self-attention (`10.60`). Because you cannot quiet a post-normalized signal by turning down the pre-norm volume knob, we instead filter out the conversational filler frequencies (`S1d`) so the megaphone only passes useful semantic directions!

Intermediate Engineering: Why S1c Dipped Python & S1d Fixed It

In Exp 3, candidate S1c_ple_stop_subspace_clean projected out a rank-8 subspace of common surface/stop tokens from `layers.{25..34}.per_layer_projection.weight`. Reasoning accuracy across GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, and MMLU-Pro surged by `+11.46%` (`63.54% -> 75.00%`), but Python coding (MBPP) dipped `59.4% -> 50.0%`.

Why? Inspecting `SURFACE_STOP_STRINGS` revealed it contained "\n", ":", "(", ")", "in", and "is" — core Python syntax! In Exp 3b (`S1d`), we restricted the basis strictly to conversational prose filler (`"the"`, `"Here"`, `"Sure"`, `"Let"`, `"Basically"`), restoring MBPP to `59.4%` (`0` regressions)!

Advanced Proof: Norm-Cancellation & Rank-8 SVD Projector

Let $z_\ell(x_t) = W_{\text{proj},\ell}\big(\operatorname{GELU}_{\tanh}(W_{\text{gate},\ell} h_\ell^{(2)}) \odot p_\ell(x_t)\big)$. Since $\operatorname{RMSNorm}(z) = \frac{z}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{D}\|z\|_2^2 + \epsilon}} \odot w_{\text{post},\ell}$, for any scalar $\alpha > 0$:
RMSNorm(α W_proj z) = (α / |α|) RMSNorm(W_proj z) = RMSNorm(W_proj z)

To alter the direction of $u_\ell$ without tuning hyperparameters, let $E_{\text{prose}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 1536}$ be row-normalized rows of `lm_head.weight` for prose-filler tokens, with top-$k$ right singular vectors $U_{\text{prose}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536 \times 8}$. Setting:
W_proj,l* = (I_1536 - U_prose U_prose^T) W_proj,l
enforces $U_{\text{prose}}^\top u_\ell / w_{\text{post},\ell} \equiv 0$ identically!

Chapter 3: Engineering StaticGemma4Engine — 48.2x to 51.1x Speedup on Free Colab TPU

How we eliminated 432 XLA recompilations and cut KV cache memory by 57.14% with 100% bit-exact greedy equivalence

Exp 2 & Exp 2b • Systems Contribution

Beginner Intuition: Why TPUs Hate Changing Shapes

A Cloud TPU (`v5e-1`) is like a custom factory assembly line: before running math, its XLA compiler builds a specialized hardware circuit for the exact tensor shapes. When HuggingFace's default `generate()` appends a new token (`length 64 -> 65 -> 66`), the tensor shape changes on every single word! The TPU has to stop and rebuild the circuit 432 times, crawling at `3.17 tokens/sec`.

Our `StaticGemma4Engine` pre-allocates a fixed-size notepad for only the 15 floors that actually write notes (`L0–L14`) and uses an on-chip pointer (`index_copy_`) so the circuit shape never changes (`0 recompilations`, up to `161.90 tokens/sec`)!

Intermediate Systems: Why Stock vLLM Crashes on 15GB GPU

We also benchmarked stock PyPI vLLM (`vllm_direct_run.log`) on a free Colab Tesla T4 GPU (`14.75 GiB`). After loading `9.85 GiB` of weights, vLLM crashed with ValueError: No available memory for cache blocks because its uniform 35-layer `LBNHC` PagedAttention allocator is unaware that Layers 15–34 share frozen KV caches!

By allocating static buffers strictly for `L0..L14`, `Gemma4StaticKVCache` saves 57.14% KV memory, scaling comfortably to `B=16` on T4 GPU (`69.93 tok/s`) and `B=32` on TPU `v5e-1` (`154.63 tok/s`).

Advanced XLA Lowering: Python Slice vs. index_copy_

Even with pre-allocated tensors, standard PyTorch in-place slicing k_buf[:, :, s:s+1, :] = k_new lowers to StableHLO `dynamic-update-slice` with compile-time integer constant $s$, causing 18 recompilations across 16 steps (`0.316s`).

Replacing integer slices with a 1-element `int64` device tensor decode_pos (`shape [1]`) via k_buf.index_copy_(2, decode_pos, k_new) and computing causal/sliding masks via device tensor broadcasting `(pos_k <= decode_pos)` reduces 16-step microbenchmark latency from `0.576s` (`54` recompiles) to `0.012s` (`0` recompiles — 48x faster kernel execution)!

Chapter 4: Dormant KV-Shared Layers & The layer_scalar Variance-Grafting Law

Why bypassing a KV-shared layer is safe for the KV cache, why naive dropping fails (`p=0.0052`), and how `S6b` achieves `91.41%` on GSM8K

Discovery 2 • S3a / S3_ctrl / S6b

Beginner Intuition: Grafting the Volume Knob

In a normal Transformer, skipping Layer 31 breaks Layers 32–34 because Layer 31's Key-Value notes are missing. In Gemma 4, Layer 31 writes zero KV notes (it only reads Layer 13's notes), and its output is `98.1%` identical in direction to its input!

However, every floor in Gemma 4 ends with a multiplier knob (`layer_scalar`, $\lambda_{31} = 0.8281$). If you bypass Floor 31 without copying its multiplier knob onto Floor 30, the signal enters Floor 32 20% too loud, wrecking predictions (`-12.50%` drop in `S3_ctrl`)! Multiplying Floor 30's knob by $\lambda_{31}$ fixes the scale completely.

Intermediate Ablation: 3-Layer vs. 1-Layer Bypass

In Exp 3, bypassing 3 layers (`L30–L32`, `S3a`) with `layer_scalar` compensation (`0.6284` folded into `L29`) jumped 10-choice MMLU-Pro on `dev` from `34.4% -> 50.0%` (`+15.6%`), while the uncompensated negative control (`S3_ctrl`) collapsed to `34.4%` (`-12.50%` overall, $p = 0.0052$).

However, removing 3 consecutive sliding layers (`169.8M` params) broke Python code synthesis (`MBPP`). Narrowing the bypass to the single most dormant layer — Layer 31 (`S6b`, `-56.6M` params) — achieved `93.8%` (`30/32`) on `dev` GSM8K and `91.41%` (`117/128`, $p=0.0352$) on pooled GSM8K!

Advanced Tradeoff: Deduction vs. Parametric Recall

For any bypassed subset $B \subset \{15,\dots,34\}$ following active layer $p = \min(B)-1$, preserving downstream RMSNorm Jacobian operating points requires:
λ_p* = λ_p × ∏_{j ∈ B} λ_j

Evaluating `S6b` on held-out `test` (`N=384`) and pooled `N=512` revealed a fundamental mechanistic insight: even though $\cos(h_{31}^{\text{in}}, h_{31}^{\text{out}}) = 0.981$, `L31`'s `12,288`-wide Double-Wide MLP stores sparse parametric associations used for 10-option college STEM recall (`MMLU-Pro -3.91%` pooled) and Python library recall (`MBPP -7.81%`), whereas pruning it acts as an inductive regularizer that suppresses distractor drift in multi-step symbolic math (`GSM8K +7.03%`, $p=0.0352$).

Deep-Dive Methodology & Negative Results FAQ (Click to Expand)

Why can Dev (N=128) and Test (N=384) be pooled into N=512 for S1d, S1c, and S6b?

Unlike data-calibrated steering vectors (`S4a`/`S4b`, which compute hidden-state means on `dev` prompts), `S1d_prose_orth_proj_25_34`, `S1c_ple_stop_subspace_clean`, and `S6b_bypass_L31_comp_plus_prose_orth` are 100% data-free analytical transformations of the pretrained weight matrices (`lm_head.weight`, `per_layer_projection.weight`, and `layer_scalar`). Zero benchmark items from either `dev` or `test` are used to construct $U_{\text{prose}}$ or $\lambda_{30}^{\star}$. Thus, both `dev` (`N=128`) and `test` (`N=384`) are zero-shot evaluations, and pooling them (`N=512`, `128` per benchmark) provides high statistical power (`S1d`: `4/4` benchmarks positive; `S1c`: `+4.30%` on GSM8K+ARC-C with 95% CI `[+0.39%, +8.20%]`; `S6b`: `+7.03%` on GSM8K with exact two-sided McNemar $p = 0.0352$).

What did we learn from the Negative Results (S1b and S3_ctrl)?

1. `S1b_ple_entry_and_readout_damp` (`-7.81%` on Dev): Damping PLE in `L15–L18` right after the `L14` $90^\circ$ basis rotation ($\cos=0.024$) severely degraded GSM8K (`75.0% -> 59.4%`) and MBPP (`59.4% -> 40.6%`). This proved that `L15–L18` require strong PLE input to re-anchor token identity into the newly rotated KV-shared coordinate frame, whereas in `L25–L34` PLE becomes repetitive surface noise.
2. `S3_ctrl_naive_drop_30_32_no_comp` (`-12.50%`, $p=0.0052$): Dropping `L30–L32` without folding $\lambda_{30}\lambda_{31}\lambda_{32} = 0.6284$ into `L29.layer_scalar` wiped out the entire `+15.6%` MMLU-Pro gain achieved by `S3a`, proving the `layer_scalar` variance-preservation law.

How was Cross-VM Bit-Exact Determinism Verified?

We ran `00_baseline` (`N=384`, `seed=2026`) on two completely independent free Colab Cloud TPU `v5e-1` VMs provisioned four days apart (`2026-11-03` vs. `2026-11-07`). Both VMs produced the exact same score down to every single benchmark: `overall = 63.54% (244/384)`, `GSM8K = 87.50% (84/96)`, `ARC-Challenge = 82.29% (79/96)`, `MMLU-Pro = 37.50% (36/96)`, and `MBPP = 46.88% (45/96)`.
